Overview

Executive Summary

This report documents and analyses a four-month internship undertaken at Y.CO, a globally renowned superyacht management and brokerage company headquartered in Monaco, from January to May of 2026.

The report provides a comprehensive analysis of Y.CO's business environment through PESTEL, SWOT, and Porter's Five Forces frameworks, examines competitive positioning within the superyacht market, and evaluates the marketing function in the context of UHNWI consumer behaviour.

The internship was carried out as a Marketing Content Intern, with core responsibilities spanning social media content creation and scheduling, copywriting for yacht listings and editorial pieces, and market research and competitor analysis..

A placement that confirmed not only what I am capable of but what kind of professional I want to become.

Background

Introduction

The Company

Y.CO is an internationally recognised superyacht company founded in 2002, headquartered in Monaco with offices in London and Fort Lauderdale. The company provides a fully integrated suite of services including yacht brokerage, charter marketing, management, crew placement, and new build consultancy, serving among the most discerning clients in the global superyacht market.

Y.CO's brand feels modern and energetic, built on a genuine sense of adventure and community. While discretion and expertise sit at the core of everything client-facing, what stands out internally is how much the team actually cares about each other and the world they work in. Its portfolio spans explorer vessels, classic sailing yachts, motor yachts, and expedition craft.

The Role

One of the first things that was organised by the Y.CO team were inductions. I was able to sit down with the heads of each department, covering charter, management, sales, and new build, etc. Getting that full picture early on made a real difference, and it directly shaped the quality and accuracy of the content I produced throughout the internship. It also allowed me to grasp the brand identity and core values immediately.

The role also introduced me to Salesforce, Y.CO's CRM system providing first-hand professional context, which was genuinely eye-opening. Working with real client data and active pipelines in real time gave me a far more grounded understanding of how marketing strategy connects to commercial outcomes than anything I had encountered academically.

Macro Environment

PESTEL Analysis

Political

The superyacht industry operates within complex frameworks including SOLAS, MARPOL, and MLC 2006. Monaco's strong political stability provides Y.CO a trusted base. Geopolitical tensions particularly sanctions affecting Russian UHNWI clients have disrupted portfolios. Brexit has also complicated UK-EU crew certifications and logistics operations.

Economic

The global UHNWI population has grown at approximately 4–6% annually (Knight Frank, 2024), directly expanding Y.CO's addressable market. The global charter market was valued at over USD 7 billion in 2024. The luxury segment has demonstrated resilience against downturns, though inflationary pressures on fuel, maintenance, and crew wages increase owner operating costs.

Social

Ultra-luxury experiential travel has seen a structural shift clients seek curated experiences rather than ownership alone, driving charter demand. Social media, particularly Instagram and LinkedIn, has become central to aspirational yacht marketing. Younger UHNWI demographics engage with brands digitally long before committing to relationships.

Technological

Digital platforms have transformed how yachts are marketed, viewed, and booked. Virtual tours, drone videography, and high-production social content are now industry standards. CRM systems like Salesforce enable nuanced relationship management across long sales cycles. Hybrid and hydrogen propulsion create new sustainability narratives for marketing teams.

Environmental

IMO's 2020 sulfur cap and tightening Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII) ratings pressure operators to decarbonise. Green yachting solar panels, waste management, low-emission propulsion is gaining traction among environmentally conscious clients. Y.CO has a genuine opportunity to lead on sustainable yachting communications as the sector transitions.

Legal

GDPR compliance is critical given Y.CO's management of highly sensitive client data. MLC 2006 governs crew conditions internationally. Anti-money laundering regulations have intensified due diligence requirements. Yacht flagging, registration, and charter licensing vary by jurisdiction, requiring careful legal navigation across multiple territories.

Internal Analysis

SWOT & Strategy

Y.CO pursues a differentiation strategy (Porter, 1985): competing not on price, but on the quality, comprehensiveness, and exclusivity of its integrated offering. By providing brokerage, charter, management, and new build consultancy under one roof, Y.CO creates significant switching costs and deepens client relationships over time. The culture is defined by professionalism, discretion, and a genuine passion for the sea.

Industry Analysis

Competitive Environment

Porter's Five Forces (Porter, 1979) reveals a market characterised by high competitive rivalry, powerful buyers, and moderate barriers to entry making brand differentiation and relationship depth Y.CO's primary competitive advantages.

1
Force 01
Competitive Rivalry
HIGH

Fraser Yachts, Burgess, Camper & Nicholsons, and Northrop & Johnson all compete for the same UHNWI clients. Differentiation relies heavily on network depth and trust built over years.

2
Force 02
Threat of New Entrants
MODERATE

High barriers in capital, regulatory knowledge, and client trust. Digital-native boutique brokerages are emerging but lack the asset management depth of established players.

3
Force 03
Buyer Power
HIGH

UHNWI clients have extensive options and long decision cycles. A single dissatisfied client can represent multi-million-euro losses, giving buyers significant leverage over service providers.

4
Force 04
Threat of Substitutes
MODERATE

Private aviation, luxury villas, and expedition cruises compete for the same leisure budget. However, the unique privacy and mobility of a superyacht sustains strong demand.

5
Force 05
Supplier Power
MODERATE

Shipyards hold power during high-demand periods. Crew agencies and refit specialists can influence service quality and costs, though strong relationships mitigate much of this leverage.

Y
Overall
Y.CO's Position
DEFENSIBLE

Full-service model, Monaco HQ, and an established brand create a defensible competitive position but sustained marketing investment remains essential.

Branding Strategy

Y.CO operates on a premium positioning model targeting UHNWI individuals globally. The brand communicates exclusivity, expertise, and trust through high-quality visual content, editorial authority, and discreet, personalised relationship marketing. Instagram and LinkedIn proved the highest-performing platforms for engagement and enquiry generation respectively.

The brief: create content that resonates with a client arriving by sea not a tourist arriving by bus.

05
Ethics & CSR

Sustainability

Sustainability is an increasingly central consideration in the superyacht industry historically criticised for its environmental footprint. Y.CO has begun addressing this through its communications and client advisory services. During the internship, the marketing team produced content aligned with growing environmental awareness including editorial pieces on hybrid propulsion, waste management best practices, and sustainable marinas.

Ethically, Y.CO operates with rigorous due diligence in client on-boarding, given regulatory requirements around AML and KYC. Confidentiality is treated as a core organisational value all staff, including interns, observe strict discretion regarding client identities and transactions (Carroll, 1991).

Y.CO's marketing function is best placed to lead the company's sustainability communications a significant opportunity yet to be fully seized.

While a standalone CSR report does not yet exist, the marketing function could play a significant role in formalising and communicating sustainability commitments particularly to a younger generation of UHNWI clients for whom environmental values increasingly shape purchasing decisions.

07
Organisational Behaviour

Workplace Reflections

Working within Y.CO's Monaco office provided rich insights into the behavioural patterns of a high-performing, international workforce. Most colleagues demonstrated strong intrinsic motivation driven by genuine passion for the superyacht world. This aligns with Herzberg's two-factor theory (1959): motivators such as meaningful work, recognition, and achievement dominated over hygiene factors in driving daily engagement.

Team Dynamics

Despite the pressures of a seasonal industry, team collaboration was constructive and mutually supportive. Management's open-door approach contributed to psychological safety (Edmondson, 1999) junior members including interns were actively encouraged to contribute ideas and flag concerns.

Career Development

Progression in the superyacht industry is characterised by deep specialisation over time. Advancement relies heavily on relationship capital network quality and client loyalty matter as much as formal credentials, making early professional exposure particularly valuable.

Wellbeing at Work

Flexible arrangements were accommodated where possible, and the culture discouraged unnecessary overtime. This approach sustained high performance across the season a relevant consideration given the demanding expectations of the ultra-luxury sector.

Cultural Intelligence

Colleagues from over fifteen nationalities were observed across the Monaco office. This cosmopolitan environment reinforced the practical importance of cultural adaptability in building trust with an inherently international client base.

08
Final Thoughts

Conclusion

This internship at Y.CO Monaco was one of the most formative professional experiences of the BBA journey. The placement provided direct exposure to the realities of marketing within an elite luxury service firm, requiring the application of both analytical frameworks and creative skills within a demanding environment.

From an academic standpoint, SWOT, PESTEL, and Porter's Five Forces proved immediately applicable to real business challenges. The structured department inductions were invaluable providing a panoramic understanding of the business that would have taken months of daily exposure to assemble independently. Working within Salesforce connected classroom theory about CRM strategy to tangible professional practice.

The internship has also been clarifying in ways that extend beyond the anticipated. While the superyacht industry is undeniably fascinating and Y.CO's team exemplary, the placement surfaced an important self-awareness: a preference for dynamic, mobile, and client-interactive working environments over the screen-intensive rhythms of a content function.

Discovering what does not suit your working style is as valuable as discovering what does.

This is not a criticism of Y.CO or the role it is a career insight of genuine value. The internship has redirected focus toward roles combining business acumen with greater mobility, client interaction, and variety whether in events, business development, or client-facing advisory positions within the luxury sector.

This clarity of direction is itself a significant return on four months of professional immersion, and Y.CO its people, culture, and world will remain a reference point for professional standards for years to come.
