Overview

Executive Summary

This report documents and analyses a four-month internship undertaken at Y.CO, a globally renowned superyacht company headquartered in Monaco, from January to May 2026.

The report provides a comprehensive analysis of Y.CO's business environment through PESTEL, SWOT, and Porter's Five Forces frameworks, examines competitive positioning within the superyacht market, and evaluates the marketing function in the context of UHNWI consumer behaviour.

The internship was carried out as a Marketing Content Intern, with core responsibilities spanning social media content creation and scheduling, copywriting for yacht listings and editorial pieces, and market research and competitor analysis. This report also includes a breakdown of the role itself, an honest self-assessment of performance, and reflections on workplace dynamics and what the experience has clarified about my professional direction.

A placement that confirmed not only what I am capable of but what kind of professional I want to become.

01
Background

Introduction

1.1 Overview

The Company Immersion Internship is designed to bridge academic theory and professional practice. This placement was undertaken at Y.CO, Monaco, from January to May 2026. The role of Marketing Content Intern provided direct exposure to strategic marketing within the ultra-luxury superyacht sector.

Monaco, as the global epicentre of yachting culture, represents an ideal environment for such a placement. The Principality's concentration of ultra-high-net-worth individuals, its cultural alignment with luxury and discretion, and its role as host of major events such as the Monaco Yacht Show make it one of the most relevant markets in the world for a superyacht company of Y.CO's standing.

1.2 Description of the Firm

Y.CO is an internationally recognised superyacht company founded in 2002, headquartered in Monaco with additional offices in London, Fort Lauderdale, and Auckland. The company provides a fully integrated suite of services including yacht brokerage, charter marketing, management, crew placement, and new build consultancy, serving among the most discerning clients in the global superyacht market.

Y.CO's brand feels modern and energetic, built on a genuine sense of adventure and community. While discretion and expertise sit at the core of everything client-facing, what stands out internally is how much the team actually cares about each other and the world they work in. Its portfolio spans explorer vessels, classic sailing yachts, motor yachts, and expedition vessels.

1.3 The Role

One of the first things I did was a series of Inductions with the heads of each department, covering brokerage, charter, management, crew, and new build. Getting that full picture early on made a real difference, and it directly shaped the quality and accuracy of the content I produced throughout the placement.

The core of my role was copywriting, primarily for Y.CO's Beneath the Surface campaign, a content series covering everything from destination guides to interviews with captains. I researched, wrote, and published articles including 48h in Saint-Tropez and 2026 Iconic Regattas, as well as individual yacht pages for the website. Beyond the website, I also prepared crew profiles and contributed copy for yacht brochures.

The role also introduced me to Salesforce in a live professional context, which was genuinely eye-opening. Working with real client data and active pipelines in real time gave me a far more grounded understanding of how marketing strategy connects to commercial outcomes than anything I had encountered academically.

Macro Environment

PESTEL Analysis

The PESTEL framework provides a structured means of analysing the key environmental factors that influence a firm's strategic position (Hayden, 2025).

Political
  • Complex international framework: SOLAS (safety standards), MARPOL (marine pollution prevention), MLC 2006 (crew welfare and working conditions)
  • Monaco's political stability historically an asset, though FATF grey list has introduced significant new pressures across the industry
  • Geopolitical tensions and sanctions have disrupted key client demographics, particularly Russian UHNWI clients
  • US client base requires careful navigation of American maritime law and international flagging regulations
  • Brexit has complicated UK-EU crew certifications and logistics, adding administrative friction
Economic
  • Global superyacht market valued at USD 24.04 billion in 2026, projected to reach USD 50.86 billion by 2033 at 11.3% CAGR (Thakkar, 2026)
  • Global UHNWI population forecast to grow significantly through 2030 (Imberg, Shaban & Fésüs-Lengyel, 2025)
  • Younger UHNWI clients favouring charter and experience over outright ownership, reshaping service demand
  • Luxury segment resilient against downturns but inflationary pressure on fuel, crew, and maintenance costs persists
Social
  • Structural shift toward curated, privacy-first, experience-led luxury: clients seek what cannot be easily replicated or publicly accessed
  • Luxury redefined as simplicity and ease rather than excess and visibility: Y.CO's integrated model is a direct response to this
  • Growing appetite for adventure, exploration, and purpose-driven travel among UHNWI demographics (Lewis, 2023)
  • Younger UHNWI clients engage digitally first and expect brands to reflect their values, not just their net worth
  • Most effective content leads with experience, community, and discovery rather than opulence
Technological
  • Digital platforms have transformed yacht marketing: virtual tours, drone videography, and high-production social content are now standard
  • CRM systems like Salesforce enable nuanced relationship management across long sales cycles
  • SEO and digital presence increasingly important: Fraser Yachts identified as Y.CO's primary digital competitor
  • Hybrid and hydrogen propulsion creating new sustainability narratives and marketing opportunities
Environmental
  • Superyachts among the most carbon-intensive forms of leisure travel: sector faces legitimate and growing scrutiny
  • Greenwashing risk significant: sustainability claims require verifiable, substantive action to be credible
  • IMO 2020 sulfur cap and tightening CII ratings increasing regulatory pressure to decarbonise meaningfully
  • Y.CO committed to Paris Agreement 50% GHG reduction by 2030, signed Monaco National Energy Transition Pact
  • Partnerships with YachtAid Global, RMI, and ISWAN demonstrate meaningful and verifiable CSR commitment (Y.CO, 2026)
Legal
  • Monaco FATF grey list: FATF cited strategic deficiencies in AML and terrorist financing safeguards
  • Heightened AML obligations: strict Customer Due Diligence and Beneficial Ownership checks now required across the industry
  • Slower transactions, rising compliance costs, enhanced international bank monitoring of Monegasque entities
  • GDPR compliance critical given volume of sensitive UHNWI client data managed by Y.CO
  • Yacht flagging, registration, and charter licensing vary by jurisdiction requiring careful multi-territory legal navigation

Internal Analysis

SWOT & Strategy

Structure & Management

Y.CO operates with a relatively flat structure across all departments, with teams organised around service lines and a strong emphasis on cross-functional collaboration. Rather than a traditional top-down hierarchy, the culture feels closer to a family working toward a shared goal, with two CEOs who are genuinely approachable and present rather than distant or authoritative figures.

Every Monday, the Creative team holds a meeting to align on upcoming content needs. On a broader scale, the whole company comes together monthly for Energy Meetings, where each department shares what they are currently working on and celebrates the achievements of the past month. The team consistently organises events that reinforce this familial culture, from Tipsy Tuesdays and monthly birthday celebrations to seasonal gatherings like a Saint Patrick's Day brunch. Management style can be characterised as participative: initiative is encouraged at every level and informal feedback is woven into daily life.

Organisational Culture

Y.CO's culture is defined by a genuine passion for the sea, a strong sense of community, and an environment that feels anything but conventional. The team represents over fifteen nationalities, with a working language that flows fluidly between English, French, Italian, and Hungarian. The Monaco office is open plan, inspired by Google's US offices which Y.CO's CEOs visited as clients. There is no kitchen, there is a bar. Arcade games and go-karts sit alongside workstations. An M&Ms dispenser is a permanent fixture. The space is white with bold Y.CO yellow accents throughout. The dress code is smart casual, and the atmosphere signals from day one that you are expected to contribute, not just observe.

Strategy

Y.CO pursues a differentiation strategy (Porter, 1985), competing not on price but on the quality, comprehensiveness, and exclusivity of its integrated offering. The company has made a conscious strategic choice to remain boutique, growing steadily rather than at scale. Rather than marketing itself as a brand, Y.CO markets each yacht individually, treating every vessel as its own story. The emphasis is never on selling. It is on experience, connection, and trust.

For one client, instead of a standard pamphlet, the team produced a full hardcover book with photography, quotes, and interviews. It would most likely not help sell the yacht. But that was not the point.

Branding Strategy

Y.CO operates on a premium positioning model targeting UHNWI individuals globally. The brand identity is immediately recognisable: iconic Y.CO yellow, a distinctive boxed glyph, and bold black uppercase typography that communicates confidence without excess. The tone of voice is confident without being cocky, adventurous without being reckless, and luxurious without being ostentatious. It never leads with a sale. It leads with an experience.

A further layer of complexity was the requirement to write exclusively in UK English, a significant adjustment as an American. From spellings to punctuation conventions, the guidelines were strict and non-negotiable. It is a challenge I grew to appreciate, and one that has permanently shifted how I write regardless of context.

Instagram is Y.CO's shop window. Enquiries are driven by events and the website. Social media here is a brand channel, not a sales channel.

Central to Y.CO's content strategy is the Beneath the Surface campaign, a platform designed to share yachting as a subject rather than a sales tool. It covers the adventures, the crew, the designers, and the destinations. It is what makes Y.CO's content feel editorial rather than promotional, and it is the framework within which most of my copywriting work sat.

Ethics & CSR

Sustainability

Y.CO's approach to corporate responsibility extends well beyond environmental commitments. At its core, the company operates according to a set of ethical standards embedded into daily practice rather than simply stated in policy documents.

Confidentiality is perhaps the most immediately felt of these. From the very first days of the placement, interns and staff alike are briefed on the importance of discretion. Contracts are signed, expectations are made explicit, and the culture reinforces the message consistently: the identities of yacht owners, client contact details, and the specifics of any transaction are never to be discussed externally. Experiencing this first-hand gave me a much more grounded understanding of what client confidentiality actually means in practice, beyond the theoretical treatment it receives in a classroom.

On the crew welfare side, Y.CO's sponsorship of ISWAN's Yacht Crew Help programme reflects a genuine commitment to the people who make yachting possible. Crew life can be isolating and demanding, and the provision of mental wellbeing support through this partnership is a meaningful and often overlooked dimension of responsible business practice in the maritime sector.

In terms of AML and KYC compliance, Y.CO operates with rigorous due diligence in client on-boarding, a requirement that has become even more critical in light of Monaco's placement on the FATF grey list. The administrative and reputational stakes of getting this wrong are significant, and the company's approach reflects an understanding that ethical business practice and commercial success are not in tension but are in fact interdependent.

Y.CO's marketing function is best placed to lead the company's sustainability communications. An opportunity that is only growing.

Y.CO has taken a notably substantive approach to environmental sustainability, grounded in four strategic pillars and formalised through a dedicated sustainability policy. The company has signed the National Energy Transition Pact for the Principality of Monaco and committed to cutting emissions in line with the Paris Agreement's goal of a 50% reduction in GHG emissions by 2030.

Practically, this includes the use of Sustainable Energy Audit (SEA) Index and Ship Energy Efficiency Management Plan (SEEMP) tools to maximise yacht efficiency ratings, partnerships with YachtAid Global for humanitarian aid and conservation efforts, and work with RMI to accelerate the green energy transition. These are meaningful, verifiable commitments that set Y.CO apart from competitors whose sustainability credentials remain largely cosmetic (Y.CO, 2026).

3.2 Key Contributions
Copywriting & Editorial

The core of my contribution to Y.CO sat firmly in copywriting and editorial work. The majority of this was produced for the Beneath the Surface campaign, writing destination guides and experience-led articles for the company website. Pieces included 48h in Saint-Tropez and a 2026 Regatta Must-Do, both of which required extensive independent research to curate experiences specifically from a superyacht perspective, identifying hidden anchorages, privacy-first dining, and on-water activities that could only be accessed by yacht.

Beyond the website, I contributed copy for a BHB editorial feature for their luxury concierge magazine, an opportunity that pushed the writing into a different register and introduced me to the demands of producing content for an external publication with its own editorial standards. I also wrote copy for yacht brochures and prepared crew profiles, both of which required a more precise, detail-oriented approach than the editorial work.

Building and formatting individual yacht pages on the website made up a significant portion of the day to day workload. While more methodical than the editorial tasks, it reinforced an important understanding: maintaining a large, accurate, and consistently presented content repository is fundamental to a brokerage's commercial credibility.

Social Media

Social media contributions were more focused than broad. Work was concentrated on Instagram and internal content, reflecting Y.CO's platform strategy of using Instagram primarily as a brand shop window rather than a conversion tool. Content was scheduled and published using Later, with a consistent focus on visual storytelling and brand voice alignment.

Events

Towards the end of the placement, I became involved in the coordination of a LYBRA drinks event hosted by Y.CO during the San Remo Yacht Show in April 2026. Contributing to event preparation gave me exposure to a side of the business that sits outside of pure content creation, and highlighted how closely marketing and business development overlap in a company like Y.CO, where relationships are built as much in person as they are online.

Market Research & Competitor Analysis

Market research tasks involved analysing competitor brands including Fraser Yachts, Burgess, and Camper and Nicholsons to benchmark Y.CO's content positioning and identify strategic opportunities. This work drew directly on frameworks from the BBA curriculum, with SWOT and PESTEL analysis applied in a live professional context rather than a theoretical one.

Supervisor Feedback & Self-Assessment

Feedback from the team was consistently positive, with supervisors expressing that they genuinely enjoyed reading my articles and were happy with the quality of work produced. The areas I identified for personal development were speed of turnaround in the earlier weeks, and building deeper technical knowledge of the vessels themselves. By mid-internship both had improved considerably, and contributions required progressively less revision before publication.

The contrast between methodical and creative work was itself a lesson in managing motivation and knowing which kind of work brings out the best in you.

Organisational Behaviour

Workplace Reflections

Working within Y.CO's Monaco office provided rich insights into the dynamics of a high-performing, international workforce. Most colleagues demonstrated a strong sense of intrinsic motivation, driven not by obligation but by a genuine passion for the superyacht world and the community built around it. The work felt meaningful to the people doing it, and that showed in the quality and energy they brought to it every day.

Autonomy & Onboarding

One of the most immediate surprises was the level of autonomy extended from the very beginning of the placement. Rather than easing interns into peripheral tasks, Y.CO encouraged active involvement and genuine contribution almost immediately. This was both motivating and, at times, a little daunting, but it accelerated professional development in a way that a more cautious approach would not have.

Multiple Reporting Lines

Reporting across multiple lines, to the Head of Digital, Head of Marketing, Head of Events, and Head of PR depending on the task, occasionally meant managing several competing priorities simultaneously. In practice this was rarely a serious problem, more a question of personal organisation and learning to communicate clearly about capacity. If anything, it provided a more varied working experience than a single reporting line would have.

Senior Team Culture

The more senior members of the team, several of whom had been with Y.CO for a decade or more, set a notably positive tone. Their loyalty to the company spoke for itself, and their conduct was consistently professional, warm, and genuinely ethical. The one tension observed was an occasional tendency from the brokerage side to make urgent, somewhat demanding requests of the Creative team at short notice, a useful real-world glimpse into the friction that can exist between commercial and creative functions.

Life as an Intern

As an intern, I never felt peripheral. From the surprise birthday party to being trusted with external publications and live event coordination, I was treated as a valued member of the team and, genuinely, as a friend. That is not something every workplace offers, and it is not something easily forgotten.

08
Final Thoughts

Conclusion

This internship at Y.CO Monaco was one of the most formative experiences of the BBA journey, not only professionally but personally. The placement provided direct exposure to the realities of working within an elite luxury brand, requiring creative skill, analytical thinking, and a level of professional adaptability that no classroom setting could fully prepare you for.

From an academic standpoint, frameworks including SWOT, PESTEL, and Porter's Five Forces proved immediately applicable to real business challenges. The Inductions with each department head provided a panoramic understanding of how an integrated luxury services company actually functions, and working within Salesforce in a live context connected CRM theory to tangible commercial practice in a way that was genuinely eye-opening.

The placement has also been clarifying in ways that go beyond the professional. Through the day to day reality of the role, I came to understand something important about how I work best. Long hours in front of a screen, structured around a fixed 9 to 6 schedule, is not an environment that brings out my best. I tend to perform better in shorter, more intense bursts of focused work, and I thrive in environments that involve movement, human interaction, and variety. Recognising this is not a criticism of Y.CO or the role. It is a valuable piece of self-knowledge that will shape every career decision going forward.

Discovering what does not suit your working style is as valuable as discovering what does.

What did genuinely excite me was exposure to the brokerage side of the business. Watching how brokers build relationships, match clients to vessels, and navigate complex, high-value transactions sparked a new area of interest: luxury real estate. The parallels are striking. Selling a superyacht and selling a high-end property require many of the same skills: discretion, client intuition, an eye for what makes something exceptional, and the ability to connect the right person to the right asset.

Events also emerged as an area of genuine enthusiasm. Contributing to the coordination of the LYBRA event at the San Remo Yacht Show offered a glimpse of a working style that suits me far better: dynamic, social, and built around creating an experience for people rather than content for a screen.

Finally, and perhaps most unexpectedly, this internship reinforced how much the people around you shape the quality of your work and your experience of it. Y.CO is a place where friendships form naturally, where the energy of the office is something you look forward to walking into, and where connections made along the way feel like they will last well beyond the placement itself. That is a lesson no syllabus can teach, and it is one I will carry into every professional environment I enter from here.
